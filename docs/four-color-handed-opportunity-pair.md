# Handed-Off Opportunity Pair

**Status:** exact current-state refinement; strongest simple descent claim falsified.

A positive focus admissibility set

\[
A(v)=Q_4\setminus c(N(v))\neq\varnothing
\]

authorizes a **focus color commitment**. It is not a stop action.

If zero focus slack persists after one realized dual-domain action, the actual
successor supplies exactly two singleton directions. One is the source
direction sigma. The other is the **resulting other singleton direction** tau,
and tau lies in the source shared opportunity pair:

\[
\tau\in O_\sigma.
\]

Current opportunity totality derives four current controls at that successor:
two in sigma and two in tau. The handed-off refinement retains only the two
controls in tau:

\[
\boxed{
A(v)=\varnothing
\Longrightarrow
|\mathcal H(z')|=2,
\qquad
\mathcal H(z')=
\{T\in\mathcal A_{\rm dual}(z'):\operatorname{mode}(T)=\tau\}.
}
\]

`HandedOffOpportunityPairCertificate` carries no future route and no preferred
member of the pair. It records only the direction supplied by the actual
successor and the two embedding-derived controls that currently share that
opportunity.

## Double-lock result

The natural stronger conjecture was immediately tested:

> if the current successor is pivot, at least one of its two handed-off controls
> gives a focus commitment, direct geometry, or a strict decrease of the phase
> fragment rank.

The full flip-family audit killed it. One exact zero-slack pivot witness has
boundary colors

\[
(3,1,0,2,0),
\]

handed-off mode

\[
\tau=(1,0),
\]

and phase rank

\[
\Phi=10.
\]

Both controls sharing that handed opportunity remain pivot and raise the rank:

\[
\boxed{
(\mathrm{pivot},10)
\xrightarrow{\tau\text{-pair}}
\{(\mathrm{pivot},12),(\mathrm{pivot},12)\}.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{handed opportunity}\not\Rightarrow
\text{one-step directness or phase descent}.
}
\]

This does **not** weaken the handed-pair law itself. The two current controls
still exist exactly and share the successor-supplied opportunity direction.
What fails is only the attempted promotion of that permission pair into a
one-step progress oracle.

That counterexample is retained mechanically in the 154-instance flip-family
test.
